The question

What does a standard clopidogrel dose actually do in an Indian population?

Clopidogrel only works once the liver enzyme CYP2C19 converts it to its active form. Variants that impair that enzyme are far more common in South Asians than in the European populations most dosing evidence comes from. This tool builds virtual patients from published allele frequencies, runs a published pharmacokinetic–pharmacodynamic model on them, and reports what a standard dose leaves behind — then asks what it would cost to do something about it.

The gradient this tool exists to quantify

What this is

A virtual cohort generator. Genotypes are drawn under Hardy–Weinberg from published CYP2C19 allele frequencies for named populations, then mapped to phenotype by the CPIC 2022 rules.
A reimplementation of a published popPK-PD model (Jung 2024), with one transparently fitted calibration parameter and a validation panel showing every check it passes and fails.
A decision layer: how many patients an Indian confirmatory trial would need, and what genotyping would cost per event averted, in rupees.
Fully sourced. Every number in the Provenance section carries its citation and a label saying whether we read the primary source, a secondary report, derived it, or assumed it.

What this is not

Not clinical evidence. It cannot recommend treatment for an individual patient, and no output should be read as doing so.
Not a claim that genotyping prevents events. TAILOR-PCI reported a hazard ratio of 0.66 with a confidence interval of 0.43 to 1.02 and p = 0.06. It did not meet significance.
Not an AI that discovered anything. The neural network here reproduces this tool's own model quickly. It learned no biology.
Not first. PharmCAT and CPIC already interpret a genotype, and an open-source clopidogrel PBPK model already exists in PK-Sim. The Prior art section names them and states what is left over.
Every figure on this page was computed in your browser. Nothing is hard-coded from a results table, no patient data is used, and nothing is sent anywhere. Open the Trial bench and change the population or the dose to see the numbers move.

How to read this honestly

The network cannot be more correct than the model that produced its training labels, and its errors add to that model's own. Attribution explains the surrogate's behaviour, and because every input here is one you set, it functions as a consistency check that the model responds the way the pharmacology says it should — not as a method for discovering anything.

Study planning

How large would an Indian confirmatory trial have to be?

The simulated event rates feed a two-proportion sample-size calculation, so the cohort's genotype composition determines how many patients would have to be screened to randomise enough carriers. The first thing this reveals is why the one large trial that tried this came out at p = 0.06.

Power against total randomised

Against the trial that was actually run

One caution about what this calculation can and cannot tell you. The treatment effect it assumes comes from a meta-analysis of switching loss-of-function carriers to a more potent agent, and the baseline event rate comes from non-Indian trial arms, because no verified twelve-month event rate from an Indian post-PCI cohort on clopidogrel was found. Change the baseline rate control and watch the required sample size move sharply: that sensitivity is the honest headline, not the point estimate.

Health economics

What would it cost, and would India buy it?

Costs are in rupees and no figure here is converted from another currency. The threshold is the empirical Indian willingness-to-pay estimate rather than a generic multiple of per-capita income. The answer at today's prices is no, and the useful part is seeing exactly what would have to change.
